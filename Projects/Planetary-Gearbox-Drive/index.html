Project Overview

This project focuses on the complete development of a compact high-torque planetary gearbox intended for industrial automation applications.

The objective was to replace an existing spur gear transmission with a more robust planetary gear system capable of transmitting significantly higher torque while maintaining a compact package.

The engineering workflow included concept generation, CAD development, engineering calculations, Lewis bending analysis, pinion press-fit calculations, DFMEA, tolerance stack-up analysis, motion simulation, prototype validation, manufacturing drawings, and preparation for production release.

Design Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (DFMEA)

A comprehensive DFMEA was conducted during the design phase to identify potential failure modes, evaluate associated risks, and establish mitigation measures before prototype validation.

The DFMEA covered gear failure, shaft deformation, bearing loading, housing integrity, assembly errors, manufacturing variation, and long-term durability considerations.

Gear Tooth Calculation

Gear tooth strength was evaluated using the Lewis Bending Equation to verify that the selected gears can safely withstand the transmitted tangential force with an adequate factor of safety.

Lewis Bending Equation

σ = Ft / ( b × m × Y )
Variable Description
σ Bending Stress
Ft Tangential Force
b Face Width
m Gear Module
Y Lewis Form Factor

Pinion Press Fit Analysis

The press fit between the motor shaft and the pinion gear was evaluated to ensure sufficient torque transmission without slippage while maintaining acceptable assembly forces and preventing excessive stress on both mating components.

Three interference conditions were evaluated to establish the acceptable manufacturing tolerance window.

Maximum Interference

Maximum allowable interference before excessive assembly stress occurs.

Nominal Interference

Recommended production interference providing optimum assembly force and torque transmission.

Minimum Interference

Lowest acceptable interference capable of preventing rotational slip during operation.

Tolerance Stack-up Analysis

A detailed tolerance stack-up analysis was conducted throughout the gearbox assembly to verify that all functional dimensions remain within specification under worst-case manufacturing conditions.

Each tolerance study validates a critical interface affecting gear meshing, bearing alignment, shaft positioning, housing clearance, and overall assembly performance.
